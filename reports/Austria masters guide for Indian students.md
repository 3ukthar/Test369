# Austria's low-fee master's rewards an early start

Yes, Austria charges you tuition, but very little: at every public university an Indian student pays **€726.72 tuition + €26.20 students' union fee = €752.92 per semester (2026/27 figures; about INR 79,000)**, India gets no exemption, and the public universities that address the point charge no application fee. The real financial hurdle is the residence permit, which requires proof of twelve months of living money in your own name: **€15,700.68 if you are 24 or older (2026 rate)**, and an estimated €16,219 at 2027 rates, plus rent above a threshold and health insurance. The realistic intake for someone starting from zero on 10 November 2026 is **winter semester 2027/28 (1 October 2027)**; a March 2027 start is still formally open at eight universities but will not work in practice. Three things decide whether you get in: an Indian bachelor's of at least three years with **at least 60%** from an institution in the right "Section" (a classification no Austrian source lets you look up), a new in-person **VFS "competence check"** in India that eight public universities have required since 2026 and whose dates, centres and rules are unpublished, and MEA-apostilled degree documents. Non-EU application windows close months before the "5 September" dates most websites quote: the first hard deadline for October 2027 is **15 December 2026** (TU Graz/NAWI Graz selective master's), then 8 January 2027 (WU Vienna) and **25 January 2027** (FH Joanneum's only deadline for applicants from India). The things to start this week are the VFS registration, the apostille chain, an IELTS/TOEFL booking and a shortlist. The residence permit is filed in person at the Austrian Embassy in New Delhi (fee €218, statutory decision time 90 days), so you need an admission letter by about June 2027. While studying you may work up to 20 hours a week with an employer-obtained permit, and after graduating you get a one-time 12-month job-search extension, though only about one in three non-European graduates actually stays.

**How to read the labels in this guide.** Every key number carries the year it applies to. VERIFIED means the figure or date was read on the institution's own page on 10 November 2026 with the academic year printed on it. GENERIC RULE means the official page gives a recurring day and month without a year and it has been applied to 2027. PREVIOUS CYCLE means only last year's date exists. ESTIMATE means arithmetic from official inputs, not a published figure. UNVERIFIED means it was not confirmed on an official page. CONFLICT means two official sources disagree. Euro-to-rupee conversions use the ECB reference rate of 9 November 2026, EUR 1 = INR 105.1435; the rate moved between 102.14 and 112.32 over the previous twelve months ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/policy_and_exchange_rates/euro_reference_exchange_rates/html/eurofxref-graph-inr.en.html)).

## Tuition is €752.92 a semester; the money that matters is €16,000 in your own account

### Public universities charge one statutory fee and no application fee

The fee is identical on every public-university page checked (Vienna, TU Wien, TU Graz, Uni Graz, JKU Linz, Innsbruck, WU, BOKU): **€726.72 tuition per semester for third-country nationals holding a student residence permit, plus a compulsory €26.20 students' union (ÖH) fee that includes accident insurance** ([OeAD tuition overview](https://studyinaustria.at/en/tuition)). The University of Vienna states the total as €752.92 per semester and adds that you pay "from the first semester onwards" and that the duration of your studies does not change the amount ([Uni Wien fee page](https://studieren.univie.ac.at/en/studying-and-learning/financial-matters/amount-of-the-tuition-fee/students-union-fee)). A four-semester master's therefore costs about €3,011.68 in fees (ESTIMATE, about INR 3.17 lakh), plus €752.92 for each extra semester. Year labels matter here: the €26.20 is explicitly the 2026/27 figure, most pages attach no year to the €726.72, no page announces a change for 2027/28, and the one institution that publishes both years lists the 2027/28 ÖH fee as "tbd" ([FH Wiener Neustadt costs](https://www.fhwn.ac.at/en/information-for/prospective-students/costs)). Expect the ÖH fee to rise by roughly a euro in 2027/28; that is an estimate, not a published number.

India is not eligible for any country-based exemption or refund. The exemption list reproduced by the universities covers least-developed countries, including neighbours such as Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar and Afghanistan, but not India, and TU Wien spells out that other non-EU nationals "have to pay a tuition fee of 726.72 € as well as the ÖH fee from the first semester" ([TU Wien tuition](https://www.tuwien.at/en/studies/admission/students-union-fee-and-tuition-fee/tuition-fee)). Paying the lower EU rate is closed to holders of the ordinary student permit ([Uni Wien equal status](https://studieren.univie.ac.at/en/studying-and-learning/financial-matters/tuition-fee/students-union-fee/equal-status-with-eu/eea-citizens)). Older guides promising refunds "for developing countries" do not apply to you.

Application fees at public universities are zero where the point is stated at all. JKU Linz says "There is no fee to submit an application for admission" ([JKU master's admission](https://www.jku.at/en/degree-programs/prospective-students/register-to-enroll/masters-degree-programs)) and WU Vienna's FAQ says "There is no application fee" ([WU FAQ](https://www.wu.ac.at/en/programs/masters-programs/supply-chain-management/frequently-asked-questions/)). TU Wien and TU Graz list no fee on their admission pages, which is an absence rather than an explicit statement, and the absence of a fee was not explicitly confirmed for Innsbruck, Uni Graz, BOKU or Leoben. Three caveats apply. WU takes a refundable **€200 security deposit** when you accept an offer ([WU deposit](https://www.wu.ac.at/en/programs/application-and-admission/deposit)). Klagenfurt's selective MSc International Management requires an application-fee payment whose amount the research did not capture ([Klagenfurt International Management](https://www.aau.at/en/study/studying-at-aau/applying/entrance-examination/master-international-management/)). And a €50 administration fee for the University of Vienna's Computer Science selection procedure appeared only in a search snippet of a page that no longer exists, so it is UNVERIFIED; check the programme page of any Vienna master's with a selection procedure. Separately, the law lets a university that doubts a document's authenticity charge a verification deposit of up to €500, refunded if the document proves genuine and admission follows ([§ 60 Universities Act, via jusline](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/univg/paragraf/60)). Any consultancy charging you a "university application fee" for a public university is charging for its own service.

### Living costs run about €1,300 a month, and the first year at a public university totals €13,500 to €22,000

OeAD's national guideline, based on the 2025 student social survey, is **about €1,300 per month**: €450 to €700 for accommodation, about €300 for food and about €500 for study and personal needs ([OeAD living costs](https://studyinaustria.at/en/live-and-work/living-costs)). University estimates put Vienna at €1,200 to €1,600 a month in 2026 ([WU Vienna](https://www.wu.ac.at/en/programs/international-and-exchange-students/international-students/welcome-to-vienna/estimated-living-costs)) and Linz at €800 to €1,250 as of February 2026 ([JKU fact sheet](https://www.jku.at/fileadmin/gruppen/50/Exchange/JKU_Factsheet.pdf)). Graz at €1,000 to €1,300 and Innsbruck at about €1,200 sit in between, on undated pages ([Uni Graz Welcome Center](https://welcome.uni-graz.at/en/study-international/costs/)) ([Uni Innsbruck](https://www.uibk.ac.at/en/international-relations-office/student-mobility/incoming/organising-your-stay/)). Student health insurance with the public insurer ÖGK costs **€78.84 per month in 2026** and is not included in OeAD's €1,300 ([ÖGK](https://www.oegk.at/cdscontent/?contentid=10007.868713)). Age matters for someone who has already worked: youth transport passes end at 25 or 26, and Vienna's regular annual pass rises to €483.10 from 1 January 2027 according to a news report of the tariff decision ([vienna.at](https://www.vienna.at/vienna-public-transport-2027-the-new-ticket-prices-are-now-set/10583666)).

### Scholarships cover little; plan to self-fund

OeAD states plainly that it offers no scholarship for a whole degree programme and that such scholarships barely exist in Austria ([OeAD scholarships](https://studyinaustria.at/en/plan-your-studies/scholarships-funding)). The realistic exceptions are narrow. TU Graz's High Potentials scheme pays up to €17,600 over two years to master's students starting in WS 2027/28, with applications open from 15 October 2026 to **15 February 2027** and no admission needed at the time of applying ([TU Graz master's scholarships](https://www.tugraz.at/en/studying-and-teaching/studying-at-tu-graz/prospective-students/financial-matters/scholarships-for-students/application-masters-scholarships)). The Helmut Veith Stipend at TU Wien gives women entering an English-taught computer science master's €7,000 a year plus a tuition waiver, deadline **30 November 2026** ([VCLA](https://www.vcla.at/helmut-veith-stipend/)). MCI offers non-EU students automatic consideration for 50% tuition reductions, which still leaves fees far above a public university ([MCI scholarships](https://www.mci.edu/en/study-at-mci/services/scholarships-grants)). Erasmus Mundus joint master's with an Austrian partner (20 programmes in the EU catalogue on 10 November 2026) are the only route to a full scholarship, most apply between October and January, and not every listed programme still has scholarships ([EACEA catalogue, Austria filter](https://www.eacea.ec.europa.eu/scholarships/erasmus-mundus-catalogue_en?f%5B0%5D=oe_project_locations_country%3AAT)). The widely quoted Erasmus Mundus stipend of €1,400 a month is UNVERIFIED on an official page. Indian-side funding such as education loans or Indian scholarships was not researched.

## Courses live in one national database and on each university's own pages

There is no central application portal and no single database with deadlines. The official national search is studienwahl.at, run for the ministry and OeAD, covering more than 2,500 programmes with a language filter and a dedicated English-taught entry point at studienwahl.at/programmes-in-english ([OeAD find your programme](https://studyinaustria.at/en/plan-your-studies/find-your-programme)). OeAD says about 600 programmes at about 70 institutions are taught in English, a figure that covers all degree levels ([OeAD English programmes](https://studyinaustria.at/en/study/find-your-programme-1-1)). One caution: studienwahl.at was unreachable from the research environment on 10 November 2026, so its filter labels and an exact count of English master's were not verified first-hand. For universities of applied sciences, the sector's own portal has filters for degree level, language and full-time or part-time mode, and showed **94 English-language master's out of 244 UAS master's** on 10 November 2026 ([fachhochschulen.ac.at](https://www.fachhochschulen.ac.at/en/course-of-studies)).

After the databases, go to each institution's catalogue, because that is where eligibility and deadlines live. The University of Vienna offers more than 120 master's programmes, split into those with and without a selection procedure, and each links to a Master Access Guide entry stating eligible bachelor's degrees, language level and required documents ([Uni Wien master's programmes](https://studieren.univie.ac.at/en/find-your-degree-programme/masters-programmes)). TU Wien lists 26 English-taught master's, from Data Science and Software Engineering to Technical Physics ([TU Wien English-taught master's](https://www.tuwien.at/en/studies/studies/master-programmes/english-taught-masters-programmes)), and WU Vienna lists nine English-taught business and economics master's ([WU master's programs](https://www.wu.ac.at/en/programs/masters-programs)). For each shortlisted programme record four facts: whether it has a selection procedure, whether it admits in winter only, its non-EU deadline, and whether the university requires the VFS competence check.

The type of institution determines how your profile is judged. Public universities check whether your bachelor's is equivalent to their own; TU Wien, for example, has the dean compare curricula, imposes up to 30 ECTS of supplementary exams for gaps and refuses admission above 30 ECTS, with a maximum of two applications per person ([TU Wien master's admission](https://www.tuwien.at/en/studies/admission/masters-programmes/other-inter-national-bachelors-degree)). Work experience plays no formal role there. Universities of applied sciences have limited places and always run an entrance procedure with documents, tests and an interview ([OeAD application and admission](https://studyinaustria.at/en/plan-your-studies/application-and-admission)), and WU's selection weighs CV, GMAT/GRE or GPA, motivation letter and work experience ([WU application guide](https://www.wu.ac.at/en/programs/application-and-admission/your-journey-starts-here/0/19/20/2/2?cHash=8d204e5699ce88327bd9d1999745e530)). Your work experience therefore counts at WU and at UAS, and nowhere else. If a part-time or weekend UAS format appeals because you have worked, note that whether a student residence permit is granted for such programmes was not verified, and FH Salzburg itself flags that full-time versus part-time can affect the visa ([FH Salzburg FAQs](https://www.fh-salzburg.ac.at/en/prospective-students/about-to-start-studying/international-applicants/international-students-faqs)); ask the UAS in writing before applying.

## Non-EU windows close three to nine months before the semester starts

### Ignore "5 September": the binding date is the third-country application line

The academic year runs from 1 October to 30 September, with the winter semester starting 1 October and the summer semester 1 March. The dates aggregator sites quote, 5 September and 5 February with grace periods to 31 October and 31 March, are the general admission periods in which you complete enrolment ([OeAD application and admission](https://studyinaustria.at/en/plan-your-studies/application-and-admission)). Third-country nationals must file the application itself in a separate, far earlier window that each university sets. The University of Vienna warns that if you miss the application period "you will not be able to complete the admission procedure" ([Uni Wien deadlines](https://studieren.univie.ac.at/en/applying-for-a-programme/deadlines)), and the University of Graz cancels applications filed in the wrong window and blocks the applicant from reapplying for two semesters ([Uni Graz deadlines](https://internationale-studierende.uni-graz.at/en/submission-deadlines/)). An Indian citizen with an Indian degree falls under the early deadline under every definition in use, whether keyed to citizenship, country of degree or visa requirement.

### March 2027 is formally open and practically out of reach

On 10 November 2026 the non-EU window for summer semester 2027 is already closed at JKU Linz (31 October 2026) and Innsbruck (15 October 2026). It is still open or about to open at Klagenfurt and Leoben (to 30 November 2026), Uni Graz (16 November to 14 December 2026), BOKU (to 31 December 2026), Uni Wien (16 November 2026 to 7 January 2027), Salzburg (to 10 January 2027), TU Wien (to 15 January 2027) and TU Graz non-restricted master's (1 December to 15 January). WU and the UAS offer no first-semester master's entry in summer. All of these demand complete, legalised documents inside the window, most require the VFS check first, decisions take weeks to months, and the residence permit then takes up to 90 days while enrolment closes on 31 March 2027 (30 April at TU Graz). Treating March 2027 as infeasible for a from-zero applicant is the research team's inference from those dated facts, not an official statement.

The 2027 dates for TU Wien's two restricted programmes are not yet shown; the previous cycle ran from 1 December to early February (PREVIOUS CYCLE).

## Eligibility hinges on 60%, an unpublished "Section" list and a new in-person test

### A three-year Indian bachelor's qualifies, but only with 60% and the right institution

Three-year degrees such as B.Sc., B.Com., BBA and BCA are not rejected for length. The national recommendation issued by ENIC NARIC Austria with the science ministry, dated March 2026 and authoritative "from 1 March 2026 until further notice", says that Indian bachelor's degrees of at least three years from **Section 1 or Section 2 institutions with at least 60% of maximum marks** give direct access to a master's. For Section 3 institutions it allows direct access from 80% at state institutions, access with supplementary exams between 60% and 80%, and **no master's admission at all from private Section 3 institutions** ([ENIC NARIC recommendation, PDF in German](https://oead.at/fileadmin/Dokumente/oead.at/OeAD_Hauptmenueseiten/Studieren_Forschen_Lehren/ENIC_NARIC_PDFs/Asien__Auswahl___Universitaets-_und_Hochschulzugang_mit_Ausbildungsabschluessen.pdf)). A four-year B.Tech or B.E. falls under the same rule and earns no formal advantage. A graduate with 50% to 60% has no direct route.

Universities apply this unevenly, and one contradicts itself.

The single biggest unknown is your institution's Section. The recommendation names the Australian government's Country Education Profiles (CEP) as its reference tool but neither defines the Sections nor lists institutions. ENIC NARIC Austria says it "cannot provide a pre-assessment" ([ENIC NARIC Austria](https://oead.at/en/study-research-teaching/coming-to-austria-information-and-services/recognition-and-certifications-enic-naric-austria)), and the University of Graz says the same. The only direct route is the CEP India profile itself, sold as a single-country subscription for AUD 45.45 and described as a tool for organisations rather than individuals ([Australian Government CEP](https://internationaleducation.gov.au/Services-And-Resources/services-for-organisations/Pages/Services-for-organisations.aspx)); whether a private person can buy it was not tested. A UK university publishes a partial, older list naming institutions such as IITs, IIMs, Delhi, Mumbai, Pune and Anna University as Section 1 ([UWE Bristol](https://uwe.ac.uk/courses/international-study/country/india)); it is dated and not binding on Austria, so use it only as a rough signal. The suggestion that CEP sorts Indian institutions partly by NAAC grade is UNVERIFIED. The practical consequence: a graduate of a lesser-known private institution risks paying for apostilles and the VFS check before learning the degree is ineligible, so e-mail the admission office of your first-choice university with your exact university and college name and ask.

The 60% is a plain share of maximum marks. If your transcript shows a percentage it is taken at face value; otherwise the recommendation gives the formula of marks obtained divided by maximum marks, times 100. Nothing is prescribed for 10-point CGPA transcripts, so if yours shows only CGPA, and especially if you are near the line, obtain an official percentage-conversion certificate from your university. Subject match matters as much as marks at public universities, and any supplementary bachelor-level courses TU Wien imposes "will be in German" even on an English-taught master's ([TU Wien supplementary examinations](https://www.tuwien.at/en/studies/admission/masters-programmes/other-inter-national-bachelors-degree/additional-and-supplementary-examinations)). No university checked asks Indian master's applicants for proof of eligibility for a master's at home, but keep Class 10 and 12 marksheets ready because FH Technikum Wien requires the school-leaving certificate and others may ask.

### The VFS competence check is mandatory at eight universities and almost nothing about it is published

Since winter semester 2026/27, applicants who completed their previous education in India must take an in-person "competence check" at VFS Education Services before applying to the University of Vienna, TU Graz, University of Graz, JKU Linz (for starts from summer 2027), BOKU, Leoben and Klagenfurt. The trigger is where you studied, not your citizenship. Salzburg's position is a CONFLICT within its own page: one passage limits the check to three biology master's and another reads as covering all master's applicants from the listed countries, so ask Salzburg before relying on either ([Salzburg](https://www.plus.ac.at/studium/vor-dem-studium-3/zulassungsvoraussetzungen/information-fuer-internationale-studieninteressierte/vfs-global/?lang=en)). TU Wien, WU, Innsbruck and the five UAS checked (FH Joanneum, FH OÖ, FH Technikum Wien, MCI, FH Salzburg) have no such requirement on their pages and are absent from the VFS form, but the scheme expanded throughout 2026, so "no statement found" is not a guarantee.

At the appointment VFS checks your identity against your passport, checks your admission documents, and assesses English, Mathematics, Biology, Chemistry, History/Geography and Physics, plus German for German-taught programmes; the fee is "around €160" at Vienna, which appears to bundle a German placement test, and "around €100" elsewhere, paid to VFS ([Uni Wien competence check](https://studieren.univie.ac.at/en/applying-for-a-programme/international-students/country-specific-requirements-for-admission/competence-check-at-vfs-education-services)) ([TU Graz competence check](https://www.tugraz.at/en/studying-and-teaching/studying-at-tu-graz/prospective-students/admission/competence-check-at-vfs-global)). Registration is through one shared Microsoft Form that asks for your passport number, one intended university and your programme ([VFS registration form](https://forms.office.com/r/GVtNDShBqm)). The form has no city selector, no date selector and no payment step, so what happens after you submit it is unknown from public sources.

How the result is used differs. The University of Graz is the strictest: a positive result of **at least 50%** must be in hand before you apply, and "Applications without a positive Competency Check result by the time of applying cannot be processed" ([Uni Graz competency check](https://internationale-studierende.uni-graz.at/en/application-masters-studies/competency-check-master/)). Vienna, TU Graz and Klagenfurt require the check before applying but ask only for the confirmation of participation, with VFS sending results directly; JKU recommends doing it first and uploading the proof ([JKU competence check](https://www.jku.at/en/degree-programs/students/competence-check-at-vfs-global/)); Leoben tells applicants not to upload anything. Every university says the check does not replace its own review, and Vienna warns that if your documents fail the country criteria "you will not be admitted – regardless of the outcome".

The following are not published on any university, VFS or form page as of 10 November 2026 and are therefore all UNVERIFIED: test cities in India, test dates and how far ahead slots are available, the fee in rupees and payment method, duration and question format, any syllabus, the pass mark outside Graz, how long results take, how long a result stays valid, whether one result serves several universities, retake and refund rules, and whether you must bring original or apostilled documents. TU Graz and BOKU tell applicants to have documents "correctly legalised and translated" before the check, which sits awkwardly with TU Graz's rule that legalisations for its selective master's may follow by 15 August; resolve that with TU Graz if you aim for 15 December. Because the form's "Intended University" field takes a single choice, ask VFS in writing whether a second university needs a second sitting and fee before you pay. The only published VFS e-mail connected with Austrian universities, austria@vfsedu.com, belongs to a separate recruitment channel for engineering graduates run with TU Wien, TU Graz and Leoben ([VFS media release](https://www.vfsglobal.com/en/PDF/media-releases/2025/VFS_Education_Services_collaborates_with_Austrias_leading_Technical_Universities.pdf)); using it for competence-check questions is a guess.

The background explains the mood you will meet. JKU received around 10,000 third-country applications for the previous semester against around 4,000 in 2024 and cited forged certificates, agency-submitted applications and admitted students who never arrived; the students' union there reports decisions arriving after the semester had begun ([The Local, 28 September 2026](https://www.thelocal.at/20260928/jku-linz-university-adds-in-person-checks-for-applicants-educated-in-india-pakistan-and-bangladesh/)). Late admission letters, more than the test itself, are the threat to your timeline.

### Apostille, not embassy legalisation, and it must certify the university's signature

Austria accepts the Indian apostille. The Austrian Embassy in New Delhi states that Indian public documents "need no prior legalisation by an Embassy or Consulate" but must carry an apostille, issued in India by the Ministry of External Affairs ([Austrian Embassy legalisation](https://www.bmeia.gv.at/en/austrian-embassy-new-delhi/service-for-citizens/legalization)). Guidance written for Germany does not transfer: Germany objects to India's apostilles and runs its own verification, and Austria does not. For admission you need the apostille on the degree certificate and the transcripts; language certificates need none.

The Indian chain is, first, authentication by the designated state authority, then deposit with one of the four outsourced agencies MEA names (BLS International, Superb Enterprises, IVS Global, Alhind Tours & Travels), then collection. MEA accepts nothing directly from individuals, does not legalise photocopies, and charges **Rs 50 per apostille plus agency charges of Rs 84 per document and Rs 3 per page** as shown on its undated page on 10 November 2026 ([MEA apostille](https://www.mea.gov.in/apostille-menu.htm)). MEA gives no processing time; state-level fees and durations vary and were not found on any official page, and consultancy claims of four to six weeks are UNVERIFIED. Start now.

The formal trap is what the apostille certifies. The University of Vienna requires that it "attest any signature(s) by the issuing educational institution ... and not the signature of the notary or translation agency" ([Uni Wien legalisation](https://studieren.univie.ac.at/en/applying-for-a-programme/international-students/legalisation)), and JKU, TU Graz and WU say the same. MEA recognises a Delhi sub-divisional magistrate route "subject to acceptance by the concerned Foreign Mission" ([MEA list of authentication centres](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/RAC_Updated_DATA.pdf)); because that route usually rests on a notary's attestation, the state education department route is the safer choice for degree documents. Whether Austrian universities actually reject magistrate-route apostilles is UNVERIFIED. Documents in English need no translation at any institution checked; a certificate only in Hindi or a regional language needs a sworn translation made after the apostille and kept physically attached. Never remove staples, seals or stickers, since TU Wien refuses tampered documents ([TU Wien legalisation](https://www.tuwien.at/en/studies/admission/masters-programmes/other-inter-national-bachelors-degree/legalisation-and-translations-of-documents)).

## The residence permit is filed in person in New Delhi and needs four to six months

### One permit, one embassy, a 90-day decision

For any study stay over six months you need the **"Residence Permit – Student"**, not a visa. The first application "must generally be submitted in person at the Austrian Embassy in New Delhi before travelling to Austria", and you wait for the decision in India ([Austrian Embassy, Studying in Austria](https://www.bmeia.gv.at/en/austrian-embassy-new-delhi/travels-to-austria/studying-in-austria)). VFS centres handle only visas; residence-permit appointments are requested by e-mail to new-delhi-ka@bmeia.gv.at, and the embassy publishes no waiting time for them ([Austrian Embassy, How to apply](https://www.bmeia.gv.at/en/austrian-embassy-new-delhi/travels-to-austria/entry-and-residence/how-to-apply)). The embassy only forwards the file; the residence authority at your future address in Austria decides, and the consulate "cannot give any information on current proceedings nor influence the processing time" ([Austrian Embassy, Settlement and Residence](https://www.bmeia.gv.at/en/austrian-embassy-new-delhi/travels-to-austria/entry-and-residence/settlement-and-residence)). Entering on a tourist visa and applying inside Austria is not the designed route for an Indian citizen: OeAD's FAQ says a C or D visa for tourist purposes "cannot be used to apply for a residence permit in Austria" ([OeAD FAQ](https://oead.at/en/to-austria/entry-and-residence/translate-to-english-faq-zu-einreise-und-aufenthalt-fuer-drittstaatsangehoerige-studierende)).

Since 7 August 2026 the law requires a decision "without delay, at the latest within 90 days" ([§ 19a Settlement and Residence Act, RIS](https://ogd.ris.bka.gv.at/eli/bgbl/i/2005/100/P19a/NOR40277978)). OeAD warns the period "is extended by a further 90 days if additional documents need to be submitted" and advises filing at least three months, ideally six, before entry ([OeAD residence permit](https://oead.at/en/to-austria/entry-and-residence/residence-permit-student-no-mobility-programme)). There is no earliest date and no deadline for filing. After approval the embassy notifies you; you must apply for a Visa D within three months of that notice, the Visa D is issued for four months, and you must collect the permit card in Austria within six months of the notice and within the visa's validity ([§ 23 Settlement and Residence Act, RIS](https://ogd.ris.bka.gv.at/eli/bgbl/i/2005/100/P23/NOR40198397)). VFS quotes about 15 working days for visa processing ([VFS Austria–India](https://visa.vfsglobal.com/ind/en/aut/visa-type#long-termstudentvisa)). Whether the Visa D is lodged at the embassy counter or through VFS is not stated officially (UNVERIFIED); the embassy's notification will say. Working backwards from 1 October 2027, file by about May to June 2027, which means holding an admission letter by then. No published data exist on actual processing times for applications from India.

The India–Austria migration and mobility agreement in force since 1 September 2023 creates no special student track; it restates the normal permit and commits Austria only to "explore ways and means" to issue permits quickly ([agreement text](https://www.bundeskanzleramt.gv.at/dam/jcr:48210014-f019-487b-b077-e50cf4bd6115/48_18_beilage_en.pdf)). No German is required for the student permit, fingerprints are taken at application, and no separate interview is prescribed.

### Documents: the checklist is national, and the embassy's own page lags behind

The Embassy has no residence-permit checklist for India; its "Students" checklist is for visas of up to six months and its items, such as flight reservations and income-tax returns, are not residence-permit requirements. The binding list is the national one: the signed application form, passport plus a copy of all pages, a recent ICAO-format photo, the university's letter of admission, a **police clearance certificate no older than three months at application, "legalised and afterwards translated"**, proof of funds for twelve months, proof of accommodation for at least three months, health insurance, and a declaration of regular expenses ([OeAD residence permit](https://oead.at/en/to-austria/entry-and-residence/residence-permit-student-no-mobility-programme)). You show originals and hand in one set of photocopies. The Indian police clearance certificate is applied for through the Passport Seva portal ([Passport Seva](https://www.passportindia.gov.in/psp/ApplyPCC)); its current fee and whether a local-police certificate would also be accepted are UNVERIFIED. Because of the three-month limit and the apostille time, make it the last document you obtain.

Three points are in CONFLICT between official sources. On translation, the Embassy's student page says documents not in German need a certified German translation, while the federal government portal says English documents "as a rule need no translation" ([oesterreich.gv.at](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/menschen_aus_anderen_staaten/aufenthalt/3/2/1/1/Seite.120121)), Vienna's authority asks for translation only for languages other than German or English, and the regulation makes translation a matter of the authority's request ([§ 6 NAG-DV, RIS](https://ogd.ris.bka.gv.at/eli/bgbl/ii/2005/451/P6/NOR40278853)). On the birth certificate, the interior ministry's August 2026 explanation says you need one only to prove a family relationship ([BMI explanation](https://www.bmi.gv.at/312_en/60a/files/b/explanation/19-aufenthaltsbewilligung-student-erklaerung-en-bf-20261006.pdf)), while an older FH Joanneum hand-out for India lists an apostilled, German-translated birth certificate among documents the Embassy requires ([FH Joanneum hand-out](https://cdn3.fh-joanneum.at/media/2025/11/Documents-for-Admission-Visa-India.pdf)). On the fee, the Embassy's student page still says €120, contradicted by its own fee tables. Resolve the first two by asking the consular section in your appointment e-mail; apostilling the birth certificate as a precaution is cheap.

### Proof of funds: €1,308.39 a month from age 24, shown for twelve months

The 2026 amounts are identical on four official sources: **€722.58 per month under age 24 and €1,308.39 per month from age 24**, proven "for twelve months in advance", which is €8,670.96 or **€15,700.68 (about INR 16.5 lakh)** ([OeAD residence permit](https://oead.at/en/to-austria/entry-and-residence/residence-permit-student-no-mobility-programme)) ([BMI maintenance brochure 2026](https://www.bmi.gv.at/312/files/broschuere_unterhaltsberechnung_publikation_a4_2026_converted_webbf.pdf)). If your accommodation costs more than €386.43 a month (2026), you must show the excess on top, and you must also show funds for health insurance. If you turn 24 during the permit year, budget at the higher rate. A worked 2026 example from the notes: a student aged 24 or over with a €450 dorm room needs about €17,410, being €15,700.68 plus €762.84 rent excess plus €946.08 insurance (ESTIMATE; no official source spells out the insurance add-on this way).

Your application for October 2027 will be decided at 2027 rates, which are not yet published. The law fixes the uprating factor for 2027 at 1.033 ([§ 823 ASVG, via jusline](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/asvg/paragraf/823)), giving ESTIMATES of about €746.43 and **€1,351.57 per month, or roughly €16,219 for twelve months (about INR 17.1 lakh)**; a press report, not an official table, gives the same €1,351.57 ([5min.at](https://www.5min.at/oesterreich/5202606111456/pensionserhoehung-2027-so-viel-geld-bekommst-du-netto-mehr/)). The 2027 rent threshold and insurance premium cannot be derived from the sources found. Re-check OeAD's page in January 2027.

Austria has no blocked-account requirement. The money must be "in a (foreign) savings book or (foreign) bank account that must bear the applicant's name and be accessible from Austria"; alternatives are a declaration of guarantee from a person residing in the EU, certified by an Austrian notary or court, or a scholarship confirmation ([OeAD residence permit](https://oead.at/en/to-austria/entry-and-residence/residence-permit-student-no-mobility-programme)). An Indian account in your own name with an internationally usable card therefore fits the published rules. You may combine sources such as savings and parental support. On request you must prove where the money came from, with six months of statements, a declaration from your parents or sponsor, their statements and their proof of income. An unexplained recent lump sum is the obvious weak point, and a loan counts only if the money is actually available during the permit year. Whether the Embassy accepts fixed deposits or a loan sanction letter without disbursement is UNVERIFIED.

### Fees

The residence permit costs **€218 since 1 January 2026**, paid in full at application and not refunded on refusal ([Austrian Embassy, Settlement and Residence](https://www.bmeia.gv.at/en/austrian-embassy-new-delhi/travels-to-austria/entry-and-residence/settlement-and-residence)). Ignore the €120 on the Embassy's student page and the €120 to €195 figures on consultancy blogs. The Embassy charges in rupees at its current rate and publishes neither the rupee amount nor the payment mode; about INR 23,100 to 23,200 is an ESTIMATE from VFS's November 2026 rate. The Visa D costs **€195, listed by VFS as INR 20,700**, plus a VFS service charge of INR 2,410 if lodged through VFS ([VFS Austria–India](https://visa.vfsglobal.com/ind/en/aut/visa-type#long-termstudentvisa)). The Embassy explicitly warns against "visa agencies, which demand large sums of money" ([Austrian Embassy, Visa](https://www.bmeia.gv.at/en/austrian-embassy-new-delhi/travels-to-austria/entry-and-residence/visa)).

## After landing: three deadlines, a 20-hour work limit and a real but narrow path to staying

### The first weeks

Register your address within **three days of moving in**; OeAD phrases it as three working days from arrival, so act on the stricter reading ([oesterreich.gv.at residence registration](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/persoenliche_dokumente_und_bestaetigungen/an__abmeldung_des_wohnsitzes/Seite.1180200)). A new arrival registers in person with a form signed by the landlord or dorm, at no charge. Then complete enrolment, because receiving the admission letter "does not mean you are admitted": at the University of Vienna admission is complete only when the exact fee arrives within the payment period, **12 July to 31 October 2027 for winter semester 2027/28** ([Uni Wien fee payment](https://studieren.univie.ac.at/studieren-und-lernen/finanzielles/studien-/oeh-beitrag/einzahlung-meldung-der-fortsetzung)). TU Wien warns that foreign transfers can lose about €12 to bank charges, and an underpayment leaves enrolment unregistered ([TU Wien FAQ](https://www.tuwien.at/en/tu-wien/organisation/central-divisions/admission-office/contact/faqs)). With the registration form and enrolment confirmation, apply for ÖGK student self-insurance, which starts the day after application with no waiting period ([ÖGK](https://www.oegk.at/cdscontent/?contentid=10007.868713)); your travel insurance must bridge the gap until then, and the Vienna authority does not accept travel insurance for the permit itself ([City of Vienna, accepted insurances](https://www.wien.gv.at/verwaltung/einwanderung/ahs-info/aufenthalt-krankenversicherungen.html)). Finally collect the permit card in person from the residence authority, bringing the registration form, the final admission and the ÖGK confirmation. The practical order is move in, register, enrol and pay, insure, collect. The first card is valid for 12 months, or less if your passport expires sooner ([OeAD residence permit](https://oead.at/en/to-austria/entry-and-residence/residence-permit-student-no-mobility-programme)), so travel on a passport valid well beyond 2028.

ÖGK's leaflet contains a condition relevant to anyone who already holds a degree: the student rate stays available provided you do not work above the marginal-earnings threshold, and a master's following a bachelor's in the same field "does not count as a second degree programme" ([ÖGK leaflet](https://www.oegk.at/cdscontent/load?contentid=10008.751795)). Whether a foreign bachelor's counts for this purpose is not stated; confirm with ÖGK when you apply.

Housing is your own responsibility; Austrian universities do not allocate rooms ([OeAD accommodation](https://studyinaustria.at/en/live-and-work/accomodation)). The 2025 student survey puts average housing cost at €586 a month ([science ministry survey summary](https://www.bmfwf.gv.at/dam/jcr:378db906-500d-4dec-885d-894f9f2d7295/Medieninfo_SOLA_2025.pdf)), and OeAD student housing in Vienna closed applications for winter semester 2026 as fully booked ([OeAD student housing](https://www.oeadstudenthousing.at/en/news/applications-for-the-winter-semester-2026-now-closed/)). Since the permit file needs an accommodation contract of at least three months, apply for dorms in spring 2027. A dorm contract also gives you the registration signature and is the safest option when booking from India. Private-market deposit customs and official scam warnings could not be confirmed on an official page.

### Working while studying

Any employed job, even a marginal one, needs a work permit that the employer obtains from the public employment service before you start. Up to **20 hours a week** it is granted without a labour-market test, for master's students as for bachelor's; above 20 hours a labour-market test applies ([oesterreich.gv.at, studying and working](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/bildung_und_ausbildung/hochschulen/universitaet/1/1/Seite.160224)). The service must decide within six weeks ([§ 20a AuslBG, via jusline](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/auslbg/paragraf/20a)). One sentence in OeAD's FAQ, that "more hours can usually be worked" in summer and winter, is in CONFLICT with the statute and the ministry page, which contain no holiday exception; treat it as unreliable. Genuine freelance contracts need no permit, but a contract that is employment in substance does regardless of its label, and breaking the rules risks "a heavy fine or even the loss of your residence permit" ([OeAD working info sheet](https://oead.at/fileadmin/Dokumente/oead.at/OeAD_Stamm/Nach_Oesterreich/Einreise_und_Aufenthalt/Datenbank_Einreise_Aufenthalt/Infoblaetter/Englisch/Allgemeine_Info_-_Arbeiten_en.pdf)).

Do not build your budget on a job. Future Austrian wages cannot count toward the first permit's proof of funds. The marginal-earnings threshold is €551.10 a month in 2026 and, per a preliminary accounting-firm summary not yet officially promulgated, unchanged for 2027 ([KPMG Austria](https://kpmg.com/at/de/media/newsletter/tax-news/2026/10/tn-kmu-sozialversicherungswerte-2027.html)). In an employers' chamber survey of international master's graduates, 59% reported job rejections because employers did not want to file for a work permit ([Austrian Economic Chamber](https://www.wko.at/oe/news/2025-07-30-spik)).

Renew the permit in person in Austria before it expires and no earlier than three months before; a late application counts as a new first application. OeAD's FAQ saying "at least three months in advance" is in CONFLICT with OeAD's main page and the government portal; follow the latter. Renewal requires **16 ECTS or eight semester hours of study success per academic year**, continued enrolment, funds again, insurance and a credit-agency extract, for another €218 ([OeAD residence permit](https://oead.at/en/to-austria/entry-and-residence/residence-permit-student-no-mobility-programme)) ([oesterreich.gv.at](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/menschen_aus_anderen_staaten/aufenthalt/3/2/1/1/Seite.120121)).

### After graduation

On completing the degree you can extend the student permit once for **12 months to look for work or start a business**, and you can also switch directly to a work-based card without using that year ([oesterreich.gv.at, Red-White-Red Card for graduates](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/menschen_aus_anderen_staaten/aufenthalt/3/2/2/Seite.120229)). Funds and insurance must still be shown during the search year, and ÖGK student insurance ends about three months after your last enrolled academic year. The Red-White-Red Card for graduates has no points system and no labour-market test, and since 1 October 2022 no fixed minimum salary: the job must match your qualification and pay the locally customary salary for comparable Austrian graduates ([migration.gv.at, graduates](https://www.migration.gv.at/en/types-of-immigration/permanent-immigration/graduates/)). Websites quoting a fixed 2026 or 2027 salary figure for graduates are describing other card types. File the switch no later than the day the student permit expires, or you lose half-credit for your student years. After 21 months of qualifying employment within 24 you can obtain the Red-White-Red Card plus with free labour-market access ([migration.gv.at, RWR Card plus](https://www.migration.gv.at/en/types-of-immigration/permanent-immigration/redwhiteredcardplus/)). Long-term residence requires five years of settlement, with student years counted at half, and German at B1 ([oesterreich.gv.at, long-term residence](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/menschen_aus_anderen_staaten/aufenthalt/3/2/Seite.120402)). Citizenship normally takes ten years and as a rule requires giving up your previous nationality ([oesterreich.gv.at, naturalisation requirements](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/menschen_aus_anderen_staaten/staatsbuergerschaft/1/Seite.260421)).

The legal route is generous; the labour market is the bottleneck. Statistics Austria figures quoted by the Economic Chamber show that only about **one in three non-European graduates** of 2020/21 was still in Austria twelve months later, with computer science and engineering graduates staying at higher rates. In the chamber's own non-representative survey, three quarters of those who left named difficulty finding a job, and only 12% had reached C1 German, the level at which academic applicants "usually only become interesting" to employers ([Austrian Economic Chamber](https://www.wko.at/oe/news/2025-07-30-spik)). The chamber lobbies for easier retention, so weigh its framing accordingly; the leaving rates are the harder data. Unemployment stood at 7.3% at the end of October 2026 ([AMS labour market overview](https://www.ams.at/content/dam/download/arbeitsmarktdaten/%C3%B6sterreich/berichte-auswertungen/001_uebersicht_aktuell.pdf)).

## Conclusion

The research changes the usual picture of Austria in two ways. Cost was never the barrier: at about €1,500 a year in fees, a public-university master's is among the cheapest in Europe, and the binding financial constraint is liquidity, namely roughly €16,000 to €18,000 that must sit in your own account before the embassy appointment. The barrier that did not exist two years ago is procedural. Since 2026 an Indian applicant passes through a chain of gates, each with an unpublished or shifting element: a Section classification you cannot look up, a test with no public calendar, apostilles with no stated duration, university processing measured in months and a 90-day permit clock. Public universities' latest windows no longer leave room for all of it. The University of Vienna's non-restricted window, for instance, opens only on 31 May 2027, which makes an on-time October arrival tight even if everything goes well. Its admission letter is valid for two semesters, so it is worth asking the Admission Office whether a letter obtained through the still-open summer 2027 window (16 November 2026 to 7 January 2027) could be used to start in October 2027. That idea is an untested inference from the published validity rule, not something the university states.

The sensible response is to diversify by gate rather than by ranking. Combine at least one option outside the competence-check scheme (TU Wien, WU, Innsbruck or a UAS) with one early, date-certain UAS such as FH Joanneum by 25 January 2027, plus the public universities you prefer, and move the three slow items this week: the VFS registration, the apostille and the English test. Where official pages disagree, follow the statute and the dated government portal over summary pages, and budget on 2027 figures rather than the 2026 ones printed today. Decide early, too, whether you are going for the degree or for a life in Austria, because the second goal depends on German at B2 to C1 by graduation far more than on anything in the admission file.
