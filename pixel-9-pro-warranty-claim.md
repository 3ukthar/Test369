# Pixel 9 Pro (Google Store Germany): getting a newer phone or a partial refund

*Research as of 31 Oct 2026. This is general information, not legal advice. For a binding check, talk to a Verbraucherzentrale or a lawyer.*

> **Note:** This public copy has the IMEI and support case ID replaced with placeholders. Fill them in from your own records before sending.

## 1. Summary

- **The chat agent was answering under the wrong rules.** They only applied Google's voluntary manufacturer warranty (*Garantie*), which allows repair or an "equivalent" device. Because you bought directly from the Google Store, Google is also your **seller**. That gives you the German **statutory warranty** (*gesetzliche Gewährleistung*, §§ 434, 437 BGB), which gives you more rights.
- **Under the statutory warranty you are entitled to a refund or a price reduction now.** § 475d(1) Nr. 2 BGB says that if a defect shows up even after the seller tried to fix the problem (and a replacement counts as an attempt), you may withdraw from the purchase (refund) or reduce the price. You do **not** have to give Google another attempt first.
- **No law forces Google to give you a newer model as such.** But two things push in that direction:
  - Google no longer sells the Pixel 9 Pro new in the German store.
  - German courts have said that when a model is discontinued, a **successor model of equal value** can be owed as the replacement (BGH VIII ZR 254/20). There is no smartphone ruling yet, so this is an argument, not a guarantee.
- **In practice, the realistic route to a newer Pixel is a negotiated deal.** Google offers a Pixel 10 Pro or 11 Pro as a goodwill gesture (*Kulanz*) because the alternative is a refund or price reduction you are legally entitled to.
- **Time matters.** You bought between Nov 2024 and Apr 2025, so your 2-year period ends between Nov 2026 and Apr 2027. Several rules extend it, but **send the written notice this week**.

## 3. Your legal position, explained simply

### 3.1 Two different warranties
| | Manufacturer warranty (*Garantie*) | Statutory warranty (*Gewährleistung*) |
|---|---|---|
| Who gives it | Google LLC, voluntarily | **Google Commerce Ltd as seller**, by law |
| What you get | Repair, or a new/refurbished replacement. Google decides. | Repair or replacement; if that fails, **refund (Rücktritt)** or **price reduction (Minderung)** |
| Can the chat agent override it? | It's Google's own policy | **No.** It's mandatory law (§ 476 BGB). Google's own terms (section 20) confirm it applies. |

The chat agent only used the left column. Your letter should rely on the right column.

### 3.2 The key rule: § 475d(1) Nr. 2 BGB
> „Für einen Rücktritt wegen eines Mangels der Ware bedarf es der … Fristsetzung zur Nacherfüllung … nicht, wenn … sich trotz der vom Unternehmer versuchten Nacherfüllung ein Mangel zeigt."

In plain English: if a defect appears even after the seller's attempt to fix the problem, you can withdraw (or reduce the price, § 441 BGB) without giving the seller another deadline.

- It applies to **any** defect, not only the same one again. The official legislative reasoning (BT-Drs. 19/27424, p. 37) says so explicitly.
- A defective replacement phone counts as a failed attempt.
- **Weak point:** for expensive goods, EU recital 52 says the seller may sometimes deserve one more attempt. Your counter-arguments:
  - Google has officially acknowledged display defects in this model.
  - Recital 52 itself names "the same problem appearing twice" as a reason a consumer can lose confidence.

### 3.3 If Google insists on another replacement
- It must be a **new** device. A refurbished replacement for a phone bought new is not a valid remedy under the law that applies to your purchase (LG München I, 25.3.2021, 12 O 7213/20; confirmed in the government's own reasoning, BT-Drs. 21/5923).
- The Pixel 9 Pro is no longer sold new. A court may then treat an **equivalent successor model** as owed (BGH VIII ZR 225/17; BGH VIII ZR 254/20).
  - You must ask for it within **2 years of the contract date**. Under Google's terms, the contract date is the date of the shipping email.
  - If the successor's list price is less than about 25% higher, no top-up should be owed (BGH VIII ZR 190/19).
  - A Pixel 10 Pro (€999) or Pixel 11 Pro (€1,199 for 256 GB) is within that range.
  - **Caveat:** your last replacement came in a sealed new box, so Google may still hold new Pixel 9 Pro stock for warranty swaps. That weakens this argument.

### 3.4 What a refund would actually be worth
This is your strongest leverage, even if you'd prefer a newer phone.

- On withdrawal, Google may deduct a "use fee" (*Nutzungsersatz*). It is usually calculated as price × months used ÷ expected lifespan (around 48 months).
- **Better legal view:** only your use of the **replacement** counts, which is under 2 months. With a price of €1,199, that is about €50, so the refund would be **about €1,150**. The deduction can be reduced further because the phone was defective while you used it.
- **Worst case:** a court counts from your original delivery, about 18–23 months, which gives a refund of **about €625–750**.
- Either way, Google pays the return shipping.

### 3.5 Deadlines (why this is urgent)
- **Base rule:** 2 years from delivery (§ 438 BGB).
- **Extensions:**
  - not before 4 months after a defect first appeared (§ 475e(3));
  - not before 2 months after you received the replacement (§ 475e(4));
  - paused while you are negotiating with Google (§ 203);
  - possibly a fresh start for the replacement (§ 212; likely, but uncertain).
- **Burden of proof:** for defects appearing within 1 year of delivery, the defect is presumed to have existed at delivery (§ 477). This most likely runs from when you received the replacement.
- If you bought in **Nov 2024**, the base 2 years end **this month**. Send the letter now and keep proof that it was sent.

### 3.6 Two traps to avoid
1. **Don't formally "declare" a refund (*Rücktritt*) or a price reduction (*Minderung*) until you've decided.** Once declared, the choice is binding, and you can't switch. The letter below only **proposes** a settlement and **reserves** these rights.
2. **Don't accept another repair or replacement, or agree to a refurbished device, while negotiating.** Accepting could be read as agreeing to another attempt, which resets your leverage.

## 5. What to ask for

You said you'd accept **(a) a newer Pixel** or **(b) a partial refund and keep the phone**. The letter offers both and leaves the choice to Google.

- **(a) A new Pixel 10 Pro or Pixel 11 Pro** with the same storage, at no extra cost. Giving Google two models makes "yes" easier.
- **(b) A price reduction (*Minderung*).** There's no fixed formula; it should reflect how much less a phone with these defects is worth. **20–30% is a reasonable starting point.** At €1,199 that is about €240–360.
  - If you keep the phone, ask Google to confirm in writing that the display repair program still applies to it. Flicker is covered for 3 years from your original purchase.
- **Fallback, not in the letter as a demand:** a full withdrawal and refund (section 3.4). Mentioning that you reserve it is what gives your offer weight.

## 9. If Google refuses or ignores you after 14 days

1. **Decide your fallback:**
   - **Withdraw (refund).** Send a short letter stating "Hiermit erkläre ich den Rücktritt vom Kaufvertrag gemäß §§ 437 Nr. 2, 323, 475d Abs. 1 Nr. 2 BGB" and demand repayment within 14 days against return of the phone with a prepaid label. See section 3.4 for the realistic amount. You can then buy a Pixel 10 Pro or 11 Pro yourself.
   - **Price reduction.** Send "Hiermit erkläre ich die Minderung des Kaufpreises um [x] % gemäß §§ 437 Nr. 2, 441, 475d Abs. 1 Nr. 2 BGB" and demand payment of [amount] within 14 days.
   - Remember: whichever you declare is final.
2. **Europäisches Verbraucherzentrum Deutschland (EVZ)**, Kehl. Free, and handles cross-border cases because Google's seller company is in Ireland. Works with ECC Ireland. You must have complained to Google in writing first, which the letter covers. https://www.evz.de/fragen-beschwerden.html
3. **Verbraucherzentrale** (your state): individual advice for a small fee. Useful for checking your dates before you declare anything.
4. **Court:** a claim at your local Amtsgericht or the European Small Claims Procedure (up to €5,000). Google's terms allow you to sue in Germany.
   - The EU ODR platform shown on Google's pages **closed in July 2025**, so don't use it.
   - The *Universalschlichtungsstelle* probably won't take the case, because the seller is not based in Germany.

## 10. Caveats

- **No guaranteed upgrade.** I found no reliable report of Google swapping a standard Pixel 9 Pro for a newer model. It has happened for the **Pixel 9 Pro Fold** (some owners got a Pixel 11 Pro Fold in Oct 2026) and for some other models when stock ran out. Treat an upgrade as a possible negotiated outcome, not a right.
- Whether one failed replacement is enough for a ~€1,100 phone (EU recital 52) is a judgment call. Your case is strong because the defects are serious, there are several of them, and Google has acknowledged display defects in this model.
- The successor-model argument has no smartphone case law yet.
- The deadline extensions (especially whether the replacement restarts the 2-year clock) are likely but not certain. Send the letter promptly.
