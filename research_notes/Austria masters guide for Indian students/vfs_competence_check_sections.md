# VFS competence check and ENIC NARIC "Section 1/2/3" classification for Indian qualifications (state as of 10 November 2026)

All pages below were opened on 10 November 2026. Most university pages carry no date of their own; where a document has a date it is given. "UNVERIFIED" marks anything not confirmed on an official page. Method notes (browser needed, unreachable sources) are in the Gaps of the last question.

## 1. Which Austrian institutions require the competence check for Indian qualifications, for which levels, from when, and who is exempt?

### Takeaway
Eight public universities require it (Vienna, TU Graz, Uni Graz, JKU Linz, BOKU, Leoben, Salzburg for three master's only, Klagenfurt), always for bachelor's and/or master's applicants, never mentioned for PhD. TU Wien, WU, Innsbruck and the five UAS checked have no statement about it on their admission pages. No page lists any exemption; the trigger is the country where the previous education was completed, not citizenship.

### Inferences
- Non-Indian citizens with an Indian bachelor's are covered; Indian citizens whose qualifying degree is from outside the listed countries are, on the wording, not covered at the universities that say "completed your previous education in". This is a reading of the wording plus a press interpretation, not an explicit university statement.
- For someone holding an Indian bachelor's plus a later EU/EEA degree, the wording is ambiguous ("previous education"): no page says which qualification counts.
- JKU's check applies only to programmes without an admission procedure; JKU master's with their own admission procedure are, on the wording, outside it. TU Graz, by contrast, explicitly includes master's with admission procedure. Vienna's check covers programmes with entrance or selection procedures too (the page says it "does not replace" them).
- An applicant targeting TU Wien, WU, Innsbruck or a UAS currently needs no VFS check, but "no statement found" is not a guarantee: the scheme has expanded during 2026 (Uni Graz and Klagenfurt went from 3 to 11–12 countries; JKU joined for summer 2027).

### Gaps
- No page states an exemption for holders of EU/EEA degrees, already-enrolled students, students changing programme, or applicants already in Austria (except Leoben's alternative venue). An applicant in one of these situations has to ask the admission office in writing.
- University of Vienna's current page no longer states the start semester (older versions and press say winter semester 2026/27).
- Klagenfurt's bachelor's page was not opened; the master's page was.
- Medical universities, art universities, University of Innsbruck sub-pages, and UAS other than the five listed were not checked.

## 2. VFS Education Services logistics: registration, centres, dates, fee, payment, what to bring, format, pass mark, results, validity, reuse, retakes, refunds

### Takeaway
The only published procedure is: fill in one shared Microsoft Form (name, passport number, intended university and programme, test type), pay roughly €100 (Vienna: roughly €160) to VFS, attend in person, and VFS sends the result to the university. Test cities, dates, INR fee, payment method, duration, question format, result turnaround, validity, reuse across universities, retake and refund rules are not published anywhere official as of 10 November 2026.

### Inferences
- Because the form collects no slot, city or payment, VFS must contact registrants afterwards (by e-mail or phone) with the appointment and payment instructions. The steps after submitting the form are therefore unknown from public sources.

- The €160 at Vienna versus €100 elsewhere probably reflects the bundled onSET; whether a Vienna applicant to an English-taught master's pays €160 or about €100 is not stated.

- austria@vfsedu.com is the only published VFS e-mail address connected with Austrian universities; it is documented for the Focus India programme, not for the competence check. Using it for competence-check questions is a guess.

## 3. Is the check a pass/fail gate before application, and what does each university say about timing?

### Takeaway
Only the University of Graz makes a positive result a hard precondition for submitting an application. Vienna, TU Graz, Klagenfurt and Salzburg require the check to be taken before applying but ask only for the confirmation of participation (or nothing), with the result sent later by VFS. JKU treats the check as part of the admission procedure and only recommends doing it first. No university gives a "book X weeks ahead" figure.

### Cited Findings
- University of Vienna: "Before applying for admission, you must complete a competence check." The applicant uploads the confirmation of participation; the university then reviews "your documents and the results of your competence check and onSET ... This may take several weeks." "Only take the competence check and onSET if you meet all admission requirements." If the country requirements are not met "you will not be admitted – regardless of the outcome" — [Uni Wien](https://studieren.univie.ac.at/en/applying-for-a-programme/international-students/country-specific-requirements-for-admission/competence-check-at-vfs-education-services)
- TU Graz: "you need to take a competence check at VFS Global before submitting an application for admission"; "As part of the registration process, you must upload your confirmation of participation"; "The decisions regarding admission to your chosen degree programme remain independent of the competence check at VFS Global"; review "may take several weeks" — [TU Graz](https://www.tugraz.at/en/studying-and-teaching/studying-at-tu-graz/prospective-students/admission/competence-check-at-vfs-global)
- University of Graz: "Applications without a positive Competency Check result by the time of applying cannot be processed! You should therefore plan your Competency Check in good time." Review after application "may take several weeks to months". "Meeting these requirements and passing the Competency Check do not guarantee admission" — [Uni Graz](https://internationale-studierende.uni-graz.at/en/application-masters-studies/competency-check-master/)
- JKU: the check is taken "within the framework of the procedure that starts as soon as you submit the application for admission. You should actually make the competence check ... before submitting an application for admission, and upload the proof when applying. Please pay attention to the application deadlines"; review "may take several weeks" — [JKU](https://www.jku.at/en/degree-programs/students/competence-check-at-vfs-global/)
- BOKU: "you need to take a competence check at VFS Education Services before submitting an application for admission"; results sent directly; review "may take several weeks" — [BOKU](https://boku.ac.at/en/studienservices/themen/zulassung/international-educational-background/masters-programmes/admission-to-a-masters-programme-with-international-educational-background/country-of-education/pakistan-bangladesh-india-nigeria)
- Leoben: the check is "part of your application for admission"; no upload; review "may take several weeks" — [Leoben](https://ssc.unileoben.ac.at/en/international-students/competence-check-at-vfs-education-services)
- Klagenfurt: "Before submitting an application for admission to a degree programme you will need to take a competence check"; confirmation is a required document — [Klagenfurt](https://www.aau.at/en/study/studying-at-aau/applying/entrance-examination/master-international-management/)
- Every university states the check does not replace the admission office's formal review, entrance or selection procedures, language proof, or supplementary examinations — pages above.
- University of Graz adds that it cannot pre-assess eligibility: "The university can only verify eligibility for university admission on the basis of a complete application. It is not possible to provide a preliminary assessment." — [Uni Graz master's eligibility](https://internationale-studierende.uni-graz.at/en/application-masters-studies/allgemeine-universitaetsreife-masterzulassung/)

### Inferences
- For the 15 December 2026 TU Graz deadline, the applicant needs to have sat the check and hold the confirmation of participation by the day of submission; the score itself can arrive afterwards. With five weeks left and no published slot calendar, registering on the form immediately is the only way to find out whether this is feasible.
- For Uni Graz the timeline is longer: the result (not just attendance) must be in hand before the application window closes, and the result turnaround is unpublished.
- For JKU (6 February–31 March 2027) and Vienna (1 March–6 April and 31 May–28 June 2027) there is enough calendar time, but the unknown slot availability argues for sitting the check in December 2026 or January 2027.
- FH Joanneum (25 January 2027) has no VFS requirement on its page, so the check is not on its critical path.
- At Graz the check is a scored gate (50%). At the other universities the result is one input to the admission decision and the threshold is not disclosed; treating 50% as the working target is reasonable but not confirmed.

### Gaps
- No university publishes a lead time for booking, or says what happens if the VFS result arrives after the application deadline.
- Whether the "confirmation of participation" is issued on the day of the test is not stated.

## 4. The ENIC NARIC Austria recommendation on Indian qualifications: wording, Sections, reference tool, percentage conversion

### Takeaway
The recommendation exists as an 8-page German PDF ("Stand: März 2026", authoritative from 1 March 2026). It names the Australian government's Country Education Profiles (CEP) as the "Hilfsinstrument" for the Section classification but does not define the Sections or list institutions. It works purely in percentages of maximum marks, gives a formula for computing them, and says nothing about CGPA scales, NAAC, NIRF, IITs/NITs, deemed or autonomous institutions, or the 4-year NEP degree. Universities apply it unevenly: the Section 3 state-university threshold is 80% at Vienna and TU Graz, 70% at Salzburg, 60% at Leoben, and Uni Graz excludes Section 3 altogether.

### Inferences
- "Section 1/2/3" is the Australian CEP grouping of Indian institutions, adopted by ENIC NARIC Austria. Earlier research's guess is correct. The Austrian document adds its own percentage thresholds on top; these are not the Australian thresholds.
- 60% is a plain arithmetic share of maximum marks, not a label such as "First Class". A transcript that states a percentage is taken at face value; otherwise the formula is applied to the marks.
- For 10-point CGPA transcripts nothing is prescribed. The formula implies CGPA ÷ 10 × 100 if the university treats grade points as marks, but a university could instead use the conversion printed on the transcript or issued by the Indian university. An applicant with a CGPA-only transcript near the 6.0/8.0 line should obtain an official percentage-conversion certificate from their university.
- The instruction to check "the continuous affiliation of the college during the study period" means the Section follows the degree-awarding university, and an affiliated college must have been affiliated to it throughout. IITs, NITs, deemed, private and autonomous institutions are not treated as separate categories in the Austrian text; they fall wherever CEP places them. The only state/private distinction made is inside Section 3.
- NAAC grade and NIRF rank play no stated role in the Austrian recommendation. Background knowledge suggests the Australian CEP sorts Indian institutions partly by NAAC accreditation grade; this could not be confirmed from an official source today and is UNVERIFIED.
- "mind. 3 Studienjahre" covers 3-year and 4-year bachelor's alike; the 4-year NEP honours degree is not mentioned and would simply satisfy "at least 3 years".

### Gaps
- The recommendation does not define Section 1, 2 or 3 and contains no institution list. The definitions sit behind the Australian CEP paywall and were not viewed.
- No Austrian source addresses 10-point CGPA conversion for India, NAAC, NIRF, IIT/NIT status, deemed-to-be universities, autonomous colleges, or NEP exit options (1-year certificate, 2-year diploma).
- JKU's and BOKU's own India percentage/Section rules were not re-opened today (earlier notes cover JKU).

## 5. Can an applicant find out their institution's Section before paying?

### Takeaway
There is no public Austrian list or lookup tool. ENIC NARIC Austria says it gives no pre-assessment, and the University of Graz says the same. The only direct route is the Australian CEP India profile, sold as a single-country subscription for AUD 45.45 and described as a tool for organisations, not individuals. Unofficial partial lists on other universities' websites exist but are dated and not binding on Austria.

### Cited Findings
- "Please note that ENIC NARIC AUSTRIA | Credential Evaluation cannot provide a pre-assessment of foreign study offers." It points to anabin and the German Hochschulkompass "no liability assumed" — [OeAD ENIC NARIC page](https://oead.at/en/study-research-teaching/coming-to-austria-information-and-services/recognition-and-certifications-enic-naric-austria)

### Inferences
- The practical options, in descending order of reliability: (a) buy the CEP India profile (about €28 at current rates; whether the checkout accepts a private individual was not tested); (b) e-mail the admission office of the target university with the exact university and college name and ask for the Section — expect a refusal to pre-assess at Graz, outcome unknown elsewhere; (c) use unofficial lists such as UWE's only as a rough signal.
- Because the Section is checked only after the application is filed, an applicant from a lesser-known private university risks paying the VFS fee and apostille costs before learning that the institution is Section 3 private and ineligible. The risk is lowest for graduates of central/state universities and their continuously affiliated colleges with 60%+, since a state Section 3 institution still has a route at 60–80% under the national recommendation (though Vienna and TU Graz publish only the 80% line, and Uni Graz excludes Section 3).
- The UWE list may be outdated (it uses the pre-CEP name NOOSR) and Section placements change with CEP updates; it must not be presented as the Austrian classification.

### Gaps
- No Austrian university publishes a Section list for India. Whether Vienna, TU Graz, JKU or BOKU admission offices answer individual Section enquiries is unknown (not tested; no personal data was sent).
- The CEP India profile itself was not purchased or viewed, so the actual Section criteria and the placement of any specific institution remain unverified.
- anabin (which the Focus India programme uses) classifies institutions as H+/H+-/H- and is a different system; no official mapping between anabin status and CEP Section was found.

## 6. Statistics, reasons for introduction, and reported problems

### Takeaway
No pass-rate figures have been published. The stated reasons, reported by ORF Upper Austria via The Local for JKU, are a steep rise in third-country applications, forged certificates, admitted students who never arrive, agency-submitted and multiple applications. The students' union at JKU criticises the extra hurdle and reports admission decisions arriving after semester start.

### Cited Findings
- JKU: "Around 10,000 applications from third countries were submitted for the previous semester, compared with around 4,000 in 2024, according to ORF Upper Austria." — [The Local, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.thelocal.at/20260928/jku-linz-university-adds-in-person-checks-for-applicants-educated-in-india-pakistan-and-bangladesh/)
- "JKU Vice-Rector Andreas Janko told the broadcaster that the university encountered forged certificates, as well as cases in which people received admission letters but never appeared at the university." - "The Austrian Students' Union (ÖH) at JKU has criticised the additional hurdle" and "pointed to cases in the current academic year where decisions arrived only after the semester had begun, by which point registration for some courses had already closed." — [The Local, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.thelocal.at/20260928/jku-linz-university-adds-in-person-checks-for-applicants-educated-in-india-pakistan-and-bangladesh/)

### Inferences
- The check is a fraud and volume filter (identity, document authenticity, basic academic ability) placed in the applicant's home country. Its timing with the March 2026 ENIC NARIC recommendation, which invites universities to test "Studierfähigkeit", suggests a coordinated response, but no official document linking the two was found.
- The universities' repeated "may take several weeks" (Graz: "weeks to months") plus the ÖH report of late decisions means admission letters may come late; this pressures the residence-permit timeline more than the check itself.
